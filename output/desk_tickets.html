Reflection

My reflection on the Problem 9 run: the three tickets (101, 102, 103) worked by the five gpt-6-luna agents on the Campus Customs Desk, starting from a freshly reset database with $3,400 in checking and ending at $152.

1 · Agent performance on each ticket

How would you evaluate the performance of the agents on each ticket and why?

Overall, each ticket was resolved correctly and without breaking a single shop rule. Every payment went through a human approval on the board, nothing was sold below cost, no purchase order went to a vendor with an unpaid invoice, and the account never went negative. Across the whole run there were zero refused tool calls, zero agent errors, and zero refused delegations in the audit trail. The agents also only pulled in the specialists each ticket actually needed instead of sending everyone on every ticket.

Ticket 101: Tauhid's Bulldog tee strong

The team handled this one well. The tee was out of stock in size S, and the real problem was that Bulldog Print Co wouldn't ship anything while invoice 501 ($840, 3 days overdue) was unpaid. The agents found that blocker right away. Accounting asked me to approve the $840 instead of trying to order around it. Once I approved it, Inventory placed a restock order for exactly one tee ($8), and after I approved that, the tee was expected by Sept 5 (5-day lead time). Customer Service kept Tauhid's reply honest the whole time. It never promised a date before the order was placed and never mentioned the unpaid bill.

The one thing I'd call out is that it took three runs and three customer drafts to get there. That's because the work really did happen in stages (pay the bill, then order, then confirm). The Boss was also honest at the end: it set the ticket to "waiting on vendor" instead of claiming it was resolved, since the tee hadn't physically arrived. I marked it resolved on the board because everything the shop could do was done.

3 · What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Ticket 102 and the quote math in 103. They were simple lookups and calculations with no real handoffs, so one agent could have done them faster.

Ticket 102 (rent): the whole job was get_rent_due(1) → check the cash → request_payment(rent, 1, 2400). That's three tool calls with one obvious answer. With the team, the Boss first read the ticket and cash, then wrote a long task for Facilities, which re-read the same ticket and cash before doing those three calls. The first run took 9 model calls and 25,398 tokens. One agent holding the rent tools could have done it in about 3 or 4 calls.

Ticket 103 (quote math): pricing the discount was just check_stock, three preview_discount calls, and create_quote, which is plain arithmetic against the pricing table ($58 list, $22 cost). Passing it from the Boss to Accounting and then to Customer Service meant each agent re-read the ticket. The quote numbers also had to be copied into the next agent's task, which is exactly where a handoff could introduce a mistake.

Why it matters: the Boss alone used about 48% of all the tokens in the run (113,438 of 236,262), mostly on reading tickets and writing delegation tasks. The team setup paid off on ticket 101, where there was a real chain (unpaid bill, then a restock, then an honest customer update), but on simple tickets the coordination was more expensive than the work itself.

4 · Three new problems this team could solve with the tools I built

Describe three new problems Campus Customs might face that this agent team could solve with the tools you built.

1. Another item goes out of stock and a vendor invoice is overdue

This is basically ticket 101 again with a different product, and the database already has a real example: the Crest Mug (CC-MUG-CREST) is at 0 on hand. If a customer orders mugs and Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3-day lead time) has an overdue invoice, the team would:

  • have Inventory find the shortfall with check_stock
  • have Inventory match the vendor by specialty with get_vendor_status
  • have Accounting request the overdue invoice payment for my approval with request_payment(kind=invoice)
  • have Inventory place the restock with create_purchase_order once the bill is paid
  • have Customer Service draft an honest "back in about 3 days" reply

The pay step still refuses to go below $0.

2. Another bill comes due, like utilities or a second lease

This is ticket 102 again. If the bill is recorded as a lease row (a storage unit or second shop space) or as an invoice row from a utility vendor:

  • Facilities or Accounting would confirm the amount and due date (get_rent_due / get_vendor_status)

3. Another club asks for a bulk discount
